01

Summary

Most people moving in London search the few areas they already know. A couple with two jobs in different places has no easy way to see every area that works for both of them, or to find somewhere with the same feel as the place they love at a better price. Property portals answer which property. Maloca answers which area first, then helps the household view, score and choose together.

The app is built and in Apple's beta review. It maps every London area within a shared commute for up to four people, using real journey times. It suggests areas like the ones a household already loves, from measured data rather than opinion. Its Ask feature answers questions about any area, draws the route to work, and plans a day out there. Its Viewings tab saves Rightmove listings, calls the agent and scores each property against the household's must-haves.

Maloca will make money in three ways, in this order: a paid Maloca Plus pass for the months of a search; disclosed introductions to mortgage brokers and conveyancers at the moment a household is ready to offer; and later, work for employers relocating staff to London. In the base case below it covers its own costs from year one, because it costs very little to run, with partner introductions as the largest and least proven line of income.

02

The problem

Choosing where to live in London is one of the largest decisions most people make, and it is usually made with very little information. The average first-time purchase in London was about £422,660 in 2024, and London first-time buyers are older than anywhere else in the UK, at around 35.

  • Two commutes, one home. A couple working in Canary Wharf and Holborn can live in dozens of areas. Most portals measure the journey to one place at a time.
  • People stay where they know. Without a picture of other areas' character, households keep searching the same three postcodes and miss better value a few stops away.
  • The information is scattered. Crime, schools, sold prices, parks and high streets live on separate government and commercial sites, each a separate search.
  • Viewings are chaotic. Listings, agent numbers, viewing times and each person's opinion of each property end up across texts, notes and spreadsheets.

Maloca was built from this experience. Its founder, Nick, and his partner Harriet were renting in Clapham Junction, working in Canary Wharf and Holborn, and had only ever known south-west London. They wanted every area that worked for both commutes, filtered by how they actually live, and a way to plan and compare viewings together. Nothing did that, so Nick built it.

03

The product today

Maloca is a native iPhone app (Android to follow), built with React Native and Expo on Google Firebase, with Anthropic's Claude for language features. It covers around 585 London areas. Over 770 automated tests guard the parts that matter most, and fixes reach phones within minutes through over-the-air updates.

Commute map

Every area within a chosen door-to-desk time for up to four people at once, from real TfL journey times. A slider shows the shared zone grow and shrink.

Areas like the one you love

Name the areas you like and say why. Maloca finds others with the same character using measured data on parks, high streets, nightlife, schools, footfall and prices.

Ask Maloca

Questions about any area, answered from the app's own data: a route map to work, a planned day out with real places, rankings of your areas, side-by-side comparisons and suggested follow-up questions.

Viewings, together

Paste a Rightmove link to save a property, call the agent, record the viewing time, then score it against the household's must-haves. Video walkthroughs and a calendar feed are shared by everyone in the household.

Privacy is part of the product: data is hosted in the EU and the UK, there are no advertising trackers, and accounts can be deleted from inside the app.

04

Who it is for

Primary

London couples buying their first home

Aged roughly 28 to 40, two incomes, two workplaces, budgets of about £400,000 to £800,000. They search over three to nine months, care about the area as much as the property, and make the decision together.

Secondary

Renters moving, and people new to London

Far more of them, moving more often, but less willing to pay. Valuable for reach and word of mouth, and for the free tier. People relocating to London for work have no areas they know at all, which is where Maloca's matching helps most.

Competition

Commute search exists, but almost always for one destination and tied to listings. Area data exists, but as dashboards for one person. Nobody joins up the household's commutes, its taste and its viewings.

The main competitive risk is a portal adding shared commutes. Maloca's answer is depth (household taste, data-grounded answers, viewings) and speed, and Jitty's backing shows that portals' parent companies are also potential partners or acquirers.

How Maloca makes money

The core stays free, because the commute map is what brings people in and what they share. Revenue is introduced in stages.

StreamWhat the household getsPrice or feeWhen
Maloca PlusUnlimited Ask, day plans, larger households, calendar sync and video storage, for the whole search.Test a £19.99 six-month pass against £4.99 a monthAt App Store launch
Partner introductionsAn opt-in introduction to a vetted mortgage broker or conveyancer, offered when a household marks a property as their favourite. Every fee is disclosed.Assumed £250 per completed mortgage, £200 per conveyancing instructionFrom early 2027
EmployersMaloca for staff relocating to London, as a benefit for graduate and transfer schemes.Per-employee licence, to be priced in pilotsYear two

Why a pass, not only a subscription

A home search lasts three to nine months and then stops. A one-off pass matches how people use the app, avoids cancellation friction at the happiest moment, and is easier to recommend.

Why not charge estate agents

London agents pay well for sellers but little for buyers. They work in their own systems and on the phone, and will not log into another app. Maloca stays on the buyer's side.

The partner fee assumption

Mortgage brokers are paid by lenders a procuration fee of about 0.30% to 0.45% of the loan. On a typical £350,000 London first-time loan that is around £1,050 to £1,575, so a £250 introducer fee is a modest share of it. Conveyancing referral deals of up to £500 exist in the market. These fees must be disclosed to the customer: Trading Standards has warned that undisclosed referral fees risk prosecution, and introductions to mortgage advice need checking against FCA rules before launch.

Cost to serve one household

Measured in September 2026: one Ask question costs about 1p in AI fees; one area-ranking run costs between about 1p and 35p. A household that completes setup and asks around 15 questions costs about 35p; averaged over everyone who signs up, including those who drop off, about 30p. Place searches are kept within Google's free monthly allowance, and TfL's journey data is free. After Apple's share of 15% to 30%, a £19.99 pass returns about £14 to £17.

Getting customers

Now to December 2026

Launch and learn

Friends and family on TestFlight, then the App Store. The founder story and the commute map as short videos on TikTok, Instagram and LinkedIn ("we worked in Canary Wharf and Holborn: here is everywhere we could have lived"). Helpful answers in Reddit's r/HousingUK and r/london. The maloca.homes waiting list.

January to June 2027

The spring market

Most London buyers start searching between January and May. Guides such as "where to live if you work in Canary Wharf". Two or three mortgage broker partners, who meet first-time buyers early and can recommend Maloca to them.

Built into the product

Every household brings a second person

Households invite their partner or housemates with a code or QR, so each new household usually means two users. Shareable area maps and day plans carry the brand outside the app.

Year two onwards

Wider reach

Android, employer pilots, and the commuter belt (Surrey, Kent, Hertfordshire and Essex). A second city only once London's numbers are proven.

13

Funding

Maloca is self-funded and costs very little to run today. The plan is to stay that way through App Store launch and the first partner revenue, so that any investment is raised on evidence rather than on a forecast.

If by mid-2027 the app has around 3,000 households and introductions are converting, a pre-seed round of about £150,000 to £300,000 would pay for a growth hire, the spring 2028 campaign and Android. A UK company at this stage may qualify for SEIS, which makes small investments far more attractive to individual investors; this should be confirmed with an accountant. For comparison, Jitty raised about $2 million at pre-seed in 2023.
